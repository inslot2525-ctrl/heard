# Proof: do the flag rules work?

Generated by `pnpm --filter @heard/api proof` (`apps/api/scripts/proof.ts`). Synthetic data only, seed
20261101, 2000 students per cohort, 8 weeks each. The script runs the same rule code the API uses
(`packages/shared/src/rules.ts`).

**How it works.** Every synthetic student has a usual level per area (60% "good", 40% "ok"). Each week
the answer *wobbles* one step away from that with some probability, and some weeks get no reply. Half
the students get a planted problem in the last two weeks (two "struggling" weeks in a row, a sudden drop
from a usual "good", two weeks of silence, or "I'd like to talk"). The rest are ordinary.
A catch counts only when the flag names the right rule and, for area rules, the right area.

*Precision* is how many flags were real: a low number means mentors see false alarms. *Recall* is how
many planted problems were caught: a low number means students are missed.

## What this does and does not show

- **Planted problems are caught.** Recall stays at or near 100% for the four inbox rules at every noise level.
  "I'd like to talk" and "silent" are close to true by construction (the rule *is* the definition); the
  useful numbers are their false alarms.
- **False alarms grow with noise.** The cost is "repeat struggling": two bad weeks in a row happen by chance
  to students who wobble a lot. On a calm cohort about 1 in 100 ordinary students gets a flag; on a noisy one,
  about 1 in 8. Every flag shows the mentor the week-by-week history, so a chance pair is easy to dismiss with
  a reason, and dismissals are logged so the rule can be tuned in a pilot.
- **"Improved after help" is deliberately strict.** It needs two answered weeks after the help and a clear rise
  (at least +0.75, ending at 1.5 or higher). Under heavy noise it misses many real recoveries (recall about 57%),
  because missing replies and wobble both eat into the rise. It is a "good news" signal, so missing one costs little; claiming
  help worked when it did not would be worse.
- **What this is not.** It checks the rules against their own intent on synthetic data. Whether a flag meant
  a student really needed help can only be measured with real use, from mentors marking flags as acted on or
  dismissed (both are logged).
